**The Business.** General Mills makes packaged foods and pet food. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended August 30, 2026) it reported net sales of $4.4 billion, down 3% because of the U.S. yogurt divestiture, with organic net sales flat. Segments: North America Retail, North America Pet, North America Foodservice and International (source: General Mills press release, 2026-09-23, generalmills.com). The moat is brand shelf space and distribution.

**Why depressed.**
1. Volume is falling. Organic volume was down 1 point in the quarter, and North America Retail organic net sales fell 3%, with organic volume down 2 points (General Mills press release, 2026-09-23).
2. Margins are shrinking. Adjusted gross margin fell 90 basis points to 33.3% on higher input costs. Adjusted operating profit fell 11% in constant currency and adjusted EPS fell 13%, to $0.75 (same release).
3. The company guides lower for the full year. Fiscal 2027 organic net sales are expected between down 1.5% and up 0.5%, adjusted operating profit down 8% to 13% in constant currency, and adjusted EPS between $3.00 and $3.20 (same release, reaffirmed).
4. The company itself expects category growth below its long-term historical rate and a continued challenging consumer backdrop (same release).

**The Setup.** The AC crossed above zero this week, from -0.53 to +0.44, with the AO at -2.10 and already rising from -2.84 a week earlier. That is why the label is degraded and not pure. Not a buy signal, an alert. The label expires after its confirmation week unless the AC crosses again.

**Risk factors.**
1. The business is shrinking before it recovers. The guidance is for lower profit, not a turn. The signal is a price-momentum event, not an earnings event.
2. Input costs. The company attributes the margin decline to higher input costs, and the adjusted operating profit guide is down 8% to 13%.
3. Portfolio changes. The Brazil divestiture closed on September 2, 2026. The company says divestitures, currency and the prior-year 53rd week reduce full-year reported net sales growth by about 4%, so reported growth will keep looking weaker than organic growth.
4. Signal record. In this week's scorecard, 18 of the 33 names that lost signal showed no price support or deteriorated, and only 6 exited on real appreciation. A degraded cross has to be treated with that record in mind.
