The business is a known brand with a clear moat, which meets the third criterion.

**The Business.** Carrier describes itself as a global leader in intelligent climate and energy solutions. In the third quarter of 2026 it reported net sales of $6.26 billion, up 12% (organic up 15%), across Climate Solutions Americas, Europe and the other segments (source: Carrier press release, 2026-10-29, prnewswire.com). The moat is installed base, distribution and the commercial HVAC franchise.

**Why depressed.**
1. The stock fell 4.6% in the week, from $56.24 to $53.63, in the same week it reported. The market did not reward the quarter.
2. The reported GAAP result was a loss. Operating profit was a loss of $2,273 million and GAAP EPS was a loss of $3.16, because of a goodwill impairment charge (Carrier press release, 2026-10-29).
3. Part of the adjusted gain is not operating. The company recorded about $130 million of operating profit benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds, and adjusted EPS includes about $0.10 from them (same release).
4. The company itself says increased input costs and tariffs partly offset the margin gain.

**The Setup.** The AC crossed above zero this week with the AO already recovering, which is why the label is degraded and not pure. Not a buy signal, an alert. A degraded label expires after its confirmation week unless the AC crosses again, and one week of price weakness against the cross is a reason to be careful.

**Risk factors.**
1. The price went down while the signal went up. The cross is technical and the close of $53.63 is below the $56.24 reference of last week.
2. Quality of earnings. The adjusted figures include tariff refunds that will not repeat, and the GAAP line shows the impairment.
3. The data-center story is the optimism in the price. Carrier reports data center orders up about 300% and a record backlog above $8.5 billion, and expectations built on that backlog leave less room for a miss.
4. Last week's exits had a weak record: of the 24 names that lost signal, 13 had no price support or deteriorated and only 6 showed real appreciation.

**The patient investor case.** The case rests on the backlog and the guidance. The company now expects full-year sales of nearly $24 billion with organic growth of about 10%, up from a little over $23 billion in its prior outlook (Q3 2026 earnings call, Benzinga transcript, 2026-10). That is a business growing at a double-digit pace sitting near the bottom of its two-year range. The model gives no valuation view and we do not add one here.

**Radar verdict.** S2D at p4, decision week W45. The signal strengthens if the AC holds above zero next week with the AO still rising.
